Title: Advances in Cardiovascular Disease Management

Abstract:
Cardiovascular disease remains the leading cause of mortality worldwide. Recent advances in diagnostic techniques and treatment protocols have improved patient outcomes significantly.

Introduction:
Coronary artery disease (CAD) affects millions of people globally. Early detection and intervention are crucial for preventing adverse cardiovascular events.

Diagnosis:
- Electrocardiogram (ECG/EKG) for detecting arrhythmias and ischemia
- Stress testing to evaluate cardiac function under exertion
- Coronary angiography for visualizing arterial blockages
- Cardiac biomarkers (troponin, BNP) for acute coronary syndrome

Treatment Protocols:
1. Lifestyle Modifications:
   - Smoking cessation
   - Mediterranean diet
   - Regular aerobic exercise (150 minutes/week)
   - Stress management

2. Pharmacological Interventions:
   - Antiplatelet therapy (aspirin, clopidogrel)
   - Statins for cholesterol management
   - ACE inhibitors or ARBs for blood pressure control
   - Beta-blockers for heart rate management

3. Interventional Procedures:
   - Percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) with stenting
   - Coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) for severe disease

Risk Stratification:
High-risk patients include those with:
- Previous myocardial infarction
- Diabetes mellitus
- Multiple cardiovascular risk factors
- Family history of premature CAD

Conclusion:
Evidence-based approaches to cardiovascular disease management, combining lifestyle modifications with appropriate medical therapy, significantly reduce morbidity and mortality.

Title: Type 2 Diabetes Management Guidelines

Abstract:
Type 2 diabetes mellitus is a chronic metabolic disorder characterized by insulin resistance and hyperglycemia. Comprehensive management includes lifestyle interventions and pharmacotherapy.

Diagnostic Criteria:
- Fasting plasma glucose ≥126 mg/dL
- HbA1c ≥6.5%
- Random plasma glucose ≥200 mg/dL with symptoms
- 2-hour plasma glucose ≥200 mg/dL during oral glucose tolerance test

First-Line Treatment:
Metformin remains the first-line pharmacological agent unless contraindicated.

Mechanism: Decreases hepatic glucose production, improves insulin sensitivity
Dosing: Start 500mg once or twice daily, titrate to 2000mg daily
Side effects: GI upset, rare lactic acidosis

Additional Therapies:
- SGLT-2 inhibitors: Cardiovascular and renal benefits
- GLP-1 receptor agonists: Weight loss, cardiovascular protection
- DPP-4 inhibitors: Generally well-tolerated
- Insulin: For inadequate glycemic control

Target Goals:
- HbA1c <7% for most patients
- Individualize based on age, comorbidities, hypoglycemia risk

Complications:
- Microvascular: Retinopathy, nephropathy, neuropathy
- Macrovascular: Coronary artery disease, stroke, peripheral vascular disease

Monitoring:
- HbA1c every 3-6 months
- Annual comprehensive foot exam
- Annual dilated eye exam
- Annual urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio
- Lipid panel

Title: Migraine Diagnosis and Management

Migraine is a common neurological disorder characterized by recurrent episodes of moderate to severe headache.

Clinical Features:
- Unilateral location (but can be bilateral)
- Pulsating quality
- Moderate to severe intensity
- Aggravated by physical activity
- Associated symptoms: nausea, photophobia, phonophobia

Migraine with Aura:
Visual aura most common: scintillating scotoma, zigzag lines
Aura typically lasts 5-60 minutes

Acute Treatment:
- NSAIDs (ibuprofen 400-800mg, naproxen 500-1000mg)
- Triptans (sumatriptan, rizatriptan, eletriptan)
- Antiemetics for nausea

Preventive Therapy (for ≥4 headache days/month):
- Beta-blockers (propranolol, metoprolol)
- Anticonvulsants (topiramate, valproate)
- Antidepressants (amitriptyline)
- CGRP monoclonal antibodies (erenumab, fremanezumab)

Lifestyle Modifications:
- Regular sleep schedule
- Adequate hydration
- Identify and avoid triggers
- Stress management
- Regular meals

Red Flags Requiring Imaging:
- Sudden onset "thunderclap" headache
- New onset after age 50
- Progressive worsening
- Neurological deficits
- Change in headache pattern
